# POL-STL-007 — Settlement and Excess Calculation

## 1. Purpose and Definitions

### 1.1 Purpose
This policy sets how motor claim settlements are calculated, which excesses
apply, and the deductions and adjustments made before payment.

### 1.2 Definitions
- **Basic excess**: The excess shown on the policy schedule.
- **Additional excess**: An excess payable in addition to the basic excess
  under clause 3.4.
- **Gross settlement**: The amount payable for the loss before excess and other
  deductions.
- **Net settlement**: The amount paid after all deductions under this policy.
- **Market value**: The value of the vehicle immediately before the incident,
  determined from a recognised independent valuation guide.
- **Agreed value**: The value agreed with the insured and shown on the policy
  schedule.
- **Input tax credit (ITC)**: The credit a GST-registered claimant can claim
  for GST included in the cost of repair or replacement.

---

## 2. Settlement Basis

### 2.1 Repair or total loss
A vehicle is a total loss where the assessed repair cost exceeds **75%** of
its market value (market value policies) or its agreed value (agreed value
policies). Otherwise the claim is settled on a repair basis. A total loss
declaration requires Level 3 authority under POL-AUT-003 clause 4.1.

### 2.2 Agreed value total loss
Where an agreed value policy is a total loss, the gross settlement is the
agreed value.

### 2.3 Market value total loss
Where a market value policy is a total loss, the gross settlement is the market
value at the incident date, capped at the sum insured.

### 2.4 Repair settlement
Repairs are authorised at a Meridian-accredited repairer and paid directly to
the repairer. Where the claimant elects a cash settlement instead, the gross
settlement is capped at the lower of the accredited repairer quote and the
assessed amount.

### 2.5 Betterment
No deduction is made for betterment, except for tyres and batteries, which are
paid pro rata to remaining service life.

---

## 3. Excess

### 3.1 Basic excess
The basic excess is the amount shown on the policy schedule. Where none is
shown, the default basic excess is: Comprehensive **AUD 800**; Third Party
Fire & Theft **AUD 600**; Third Party Property Damage **AUD 500**.

### 3.2 Not-at-fault waiver
No excess is payable where the insured was not at fault and the at-fault
party's name, address and vehicle registration have been provided. The waiver
covers the basic excess and all additional excesses.

### 3.3 Windscreen and glass
The first windscreen or window glass claim in a policy period carries no excess
under a comprehensive policy (POL-MTR-002 clause 4.3).

### 3.4 Additional excesses
The following are added to the basic excess:
- (a) **Young driver**: where the driver at the incident date was under
  25 years of age — AUD 1,200.
- (b) **Inexperienced driver**: where the driver had held a full licence for
  less than 2 years at the incident date, whatever their age — AUD 1,200.
- (c) Where both (a) and (b) apply, a single additional excess of AUD 1,200 is
  payable, not AUD 2,400.
- (d) **Unlisted driver**: where cover is restored under the rebuttal in
  POL-MTR-002 clause 3.5 — AUD 2,000, payable in addition to (a) or (b).

### 3.5 Learner and provisional licence holders
A learner or provisional licence is not a full licence for the purpose of
clause 3.4(b). The period for which a full licence has been held is zero.

### 3.6 One excess per claim
The excess is applied once per claim, not once per payment or per benefit. For
damage caused by a declared catastrophe event, one excess applies per vehicle
per event.

### 3.7 Excess deduction
The excess is deducted from the settlement. Where repairs are paid directly to
the repairer, the claimant pays the excess to the repairer and the payment to
the repairer is reduced by the same amount.

### 3.8 Excess refund on recovery
Where Meridian recovers its outlay from an at-fault third party, the excess
paid by the insured is refunded within 10 business days of the recovery being
received.

### 3.9 Excess equal to or above the loss
Where the excess equals or exceeds the gross settlement, no payment is made.
The claim is closed as "below excess". This is not a denial.

---

## 4. Limits, Deductions and Tax

### 4.1 Settlement ceiling
The gross settlement for the vehicle may not exceed the sum insured or agreed
value. Benefits payable under POL-MTR-002 section 4 and POL-MTR-002 clause 2.6
(towing) are payable in addition, up to their own sub-limits.

### 4.2 Unpaid premium
Outstanding premium instalments for the current period of cover are deducted
from a total loss settlement.

### 4.3 Salvage
On payment of a total loss, the salvage becomes the property of Meridian.

### 4.4 GST and input tax credits
Settlements are calculated on GST-inclusive costs. Where the claimant is
registered for GST and entitled to an input tax credit for the cost of repair
or replacement, the gross settlement is reduced by the ITC entitlement
**before** the excess is deducted. The ITC entitlement is the GST component of
the cost (one-eleventh of the GST-inclusive amount) multiplied by the
claimant's ITC percentage. The claimant's GST status and ITC percentage must be
on file (POL-DOC-005 code GST_DECLARATION).

### 4.5 Rounding
Settlement amounts are calculated to the cent and rounded to the nearest cent.

---

## 5. Payment

### 5.1 Timing
Accepted settlements are paid within the timeframe in POL-CLM-001 clause 8.1.

### 5.2 Payee
Repair settlements are paid to the repairer. Cash settlements are paid to the
insured. On a total loss of an encumbered vehicle, the financier is paid the
payout amount first and any balance is paid to the insured.

### 5.3 Payment controls
All payments are subject to POL-AUT-003 section 5.

---

## 6. Recoveries

### 6.1 Recovery action
Recovery action must be commenced within **30 calendar days** of payment where
the insured was not at fault, the at-fault party is identified, and the net
settlement exceeds **AUD 2,000**.

### 6.2 Recovery write-off
A recovery may be written off only within the authority set by POL-AUT-003
clause 4.5.

---

## 7. Worked Examples

### 7.1 Young driver, comprehensive repair
Gross settlement AUD 9,000; basic excess AUD 800; driver aged 23 with a full
licence held for 4 years. Additional excess under clause 3.4(a) AUD 1,200.
Total excess AUD 2,000. Net settlement AUD 7,000.

### 7.2 GST-registered claimant
Gross settlement AUD 5,500 (GST-inclusive); claimant entitled to a 50% ITC.
ITC entitlement AUD 250 (AUD 500 GST component × 50%). Adjusted gross
AUD 5,250. Basic excess AUD 800. Net settlement AUD 4,450.

### 7.3 Not-at-fault claim
Gross settlement AUD 4,400; insured not at fault; at-fault driver's name,
address and registration provided. Excess waived under clause 3.2. Net
settlement AUD 4,400. Recovery action required under clause 6.1.
